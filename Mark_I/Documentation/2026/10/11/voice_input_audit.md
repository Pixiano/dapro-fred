# Voice input audit (2026-10-11, read-only, nothing changed)

The question: why FRED sometimes does not hear Vatsal, sometimes answers the TV, and sometimes hears itself.
Data used: `FRED_Private/events/2026-10-08, 09, 11.jsonl` (40,107 lines, bad lines skipped), `Data/clock|stt|wakeword` (1,029 clips with their jsonl), and the console output of live runs 1 to 17.
Method: the event logs give the counts. Every clip was re-measured for level, noise floor, Silero speech probability (the ONNX that ships with faster-whisper, CPU), webrtc voiced fraction, and the ECAPA score against the **current** 20 references (CPU).
The clips that contain speech were re-transcribed with large-v3-turbo (GPU), only so they could be labelled.
No ground truth exists. Labels below are mine, made from the transcript and the timing: "user" was hand-picked, "FRED echo" means 60% or more of the words match a line FRED said in the previous 60 s. Treat every count as approximate.
Caveat: the clips of 2026-10-09 include clips that were enrolled at 12:41 on 2026-10-11, so their scores come out too high. Only the 2026-10-11 clips recorded after 12:41 give unbiased voice scores.

## Headline findings

1. **The wake word fires on silence.** On 2026-10-11 there were 48 wake events. Only 7 clips hold a wake-like phrase. 27 hold no speech at all (Silero < 0.5, peak as low as 0.0001, wake score up to 1.0). Most of the other 14 hold "Mm-hmm"- or "Okay"-like sounds. On the 2026-10-09 night, 18 of 26 fires had no speech in the clip. All 13 true fires had Silero ≥ 0.85.
2. **False wakes go around the voice gate.** A wake event marks everything in the next 8 s (`wake_open_s`) as addressed. It lets a clip shorter than 1 s through with no voice check, and it lets speech cut FRED off with no voice check. Run 14 at 13:25:01 shows the whole chain:
   - a false wake (score 0.75, clip with no speech);
   - "Yeah." (0.96 s, not scored) became a turn;
   - "God, it's your name." cut FRED off with no voice check and became a turn.

   On 2026-10-11, 19 of 58 non-hotkey turns were shorter than 1.0 s, so they were never voice-checked.
3. **Many failed turns were not speech.** 23 of 64 turns on 2026-10-11 came back from Whisper as empty, and 8 of the 13 saved empty-transcript clips contain no speech per Silero. Most came from wake-opened turns. This also explains why Parakeet and Canary returned empty text on "the same clips" (`stt_research.md`): those clips are largely non-speech, so they are not evidence of a model problem.
4. **webrtc VAD mode 3 is the wrong detector for this mic.** The clock opened 829 utterances on 2026-10-11 (660 ignored as not addressed). 279 of 390 saved not-addressed clips that day, and 368 of 386 from the 2026-10-09 night (the hum), have Silero < 0.5. Speech is also cut into pieces: 51% of VAD segments are 500 ms or shorter, and each piece is addressed and voice-checked on its own ("detected" / "detected by phone" / "by phone.").
5. **The mic signal is quiet at maximum gain, and something upstream gates it.** The Windows endpoint is already at 100% (+32 dB). Speech peaks are 0.02 to 0.18 (median 0.06), RMS is about 0.007 (−43 dBFS), and the floor between words is digital zero: 8 to 26% of samples inside utterances are exactly 0, with zero runs up to 1 s. That points to a noise gate or suppressor in the USB dongle or a Windows effect (not confirmed). FRED also opens the **MME** entry (44.1 kHz, picked first by name), not WASAPI.
6. **The voice scores overlap.** Unbiased 2026-10-11 user clips (n=41): median 0.41, min 0.25, 6 of 41 under 0.33. FRED echo goes up to 0.34 (0.36 earlier), TV and other voices up to 0.33, non-speech clips up to 0.42. No single threshold separates them. Scoring only the voiced frames did not help (small n).
7. **Vatsal's own speech was mostly lost to "not addressed", not to "not Vatsal".** Of 54 hand-labelled user clips that were ignored, 48 were `not_addressed` and 6 `not_vatsal`. Clear requests to FRED that were lost: "What's 26 times 55?" (3 times, the wake word did not fire; he then used the hotkey) and "Turn the volume up a little bit" (4 times, no wake event). About 10 "Hey Fred" utterances reached the clock but produced no wake event (about 5 per day on 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-11), against 13 true wake fires. So the wake word catches roughly half the time (uncertain).
8. **Other evidence:**
   - FRED's own lines were accepted as turns: "It's 6.46 a.m. on Friday." (0.36), "That's correct, sir." (0.34), "I'm not afraid, sir." (accepted at 13:06:38).
   - One request became two turns at 13:48:03: a wake event rescued the held clip, and the "Hey Fred." clip still running ended addressed at the same moment.
   - From 13:05:55 to 13:07:12 a second listener (probably a test script) wrote doubled LISTEN events and clips into `Data/clock`. FRED's own event log has none, so this cannot be traced from FRED's side.

## 4. Design issues in the current pipeline
- `clock/turn_controller.py:189-193, 181, 210-214`: being "woke" (any wake event in the 8 s before or during the utterance) is a skeleton key. It addresses the utterance, lowers the voice threshold, skips the voice check for barge-in, and lets unscored clips through. The wake event itself has no check.
- `turn_controller.py:41` + `engine/live.py:190`: `headphones` is always True, so `barge_level` (L179) never acts. Its value (1500) does not match this mic's levels.
- `turn_controller.py:212` together with `engine/live.py:168`: the "too short" rule is skipped when woke or when the last reply ended in "?", which covers most replies.
- `clock/voice_id.py:93` and `turn_controller.py:182, 211`: the voice score runs on the capture buffer (0.5 s pre-roll, or 1.5 s for wake-opened turns, plus the trailing silence). The 1 s minimum measures buffer length, not speech.
- `turn_controller.py:93-102, 201-205`: there is one held-clip slot. A newer unaddressed clip finalises the older one, so a slow wake rescues the wrong clip. A rescue plus a running utterance gives two turns (13:48:03).
- `turn_controller.py:99`: a wake-opened turn back-dates `t_start` by 1 s and starts the capture with 1.5 s pre-roll even when nothing is said (15 such turns on 2026-10-11, none useful).
- `turn_controller.py:104-111` with `audio/mic.py:59-66`: the clock tick, Smart Turn and ECAPA run synchronously in the PortAudio callback thread, and `status` (overflow) is discarded (`mic.py:101`).
- `audio/vad.py:83, 95`: webrtc mode 3 depends on the input level. With a 200 ms hangover a sentence breaks into pieces, and the clock judges each piece on its own (addressing, `followup_min_s`, voice). Smart Turn returns p ≥ 0.9 on fragments, so it does not glue them back together.
- `turn_controller.py:84-88` + `audio/tts.py:178, 192, 208`: `tts.start` is sent before the 1.5 s Bluetooth pre-roll, so the user's speech in that silence counts as "while FRED speaks" (a short reply there is dropped as BACKCHANNEL; 34 backchannels on 2026-10-11). `tts.end` comes after the 1 s post-roll, but Bluetooth latency can still carry echo into the follow-up window.
- `engine/live.py:183`: the echo filter works on text after Whisper. It cannot stop a barge-in (which happens earlier, on audio) and misses echo of under 3 words or echo Whisper garbled.
- `audio/mic.py:96-98`: the device is the first name match, which is MME (44.1 kHz resampled by Windows), not WASAPI (48 kHz).
- `audio/wakeword.py:99`: a fire depends only on score and cooldown. The AGC gain at fire time is 9-22x and the false fires happen on near-zero input. The cause inside the frozen model is unknown; the gate in F2 sits outside the model.
- `audio/stt.py:86`: hotwords and `initial_prompt` both become prompt tokens, the same path that made Whisper repeat the hint list on noise on 2026-10-08. News names grow that list every edition.
- Tuning loop: thresholds were changed 6 times on 2026-10-11 using scores taken from enrolled clips (a self-match) or recalled by hand. Without L1 and L10 each change fixes one complaint and opens another (0.45 → 0.33, 0.38 → 0.25 → 0.27).
