📘 F.R.E.D. — Phases 11–20
"The Awakening" — Building My Own JARVIS
(Not a product. Not for sale. A machine that lives with me, in my house, on my terms.)

Why this exists:
This isn't a startup roadmap. There's no launch date, no users to onboard, no
roadmap slide for investors. This is one person building a mind to live
alongside him — something that answers when he speaks, watches over his
machine, remembers who he is, and gets better at being *his* assistant
specifically, not anyone else's. Every phase below earns its place because
it makes FRED feel more real to live with, not more sellable.

Context / honesty check:
Phases 1-10 are mostly built but not all wired together yet. STT
(audio/stt.py), TTS (audio/tts.py), and the tool registry
(tools/registry.py + system_tools.py) exist as real, working modules — they
just aren't connected to the live loop yet. "Reviving FRED" (a separate,
later step) means wiring these three in so Phases 4, 5, and 8 stop being
half-true. Phase 11 below assumes that's already happened.


Phase 11 – The Unification
⏱ 1–1.5 weeks
Tagline: "One body, one voice."
Goal: Stop being four disconnected scripts pretending to be one assistant.
Tasks:
  - Wire ToolRegistry into the orchestrator so FRED can actually act, not just talk.
  - Wire STT + TTS into main.py as a real voice mode, with text mode kept as a quiet fallback.
  - Add wake-word detection so FRED is listening, not waiting to be pressed.
  - Fix the personality/settings split — one system prompt, one source of truth, no more hardcoded duplicates.
Outcome: For the first time, FRED is one thing. Not modules. One presence.

Phase 12 – The Dispatcher (and the first commit)
⏱ ~1 week
Tagline: "Knowing what to do before thinking too hard."
Goal: A brainstem that decides instantly where a thought should go — and the moment this becomes a project with a history.
Tasks:
  - git init at the project root, first real commit of Core/ (secrets already gitignored from Phase 1 setup).
  - Route input: tool call / OS command / general conversation / live lookup.
  - Use the local model's native function-calling (Qwen/Nemotron support tool-calling) instead of hand-rolling a classifier where possible.
  - Reserve "deep tier" reasoning for things that actually deserve it.
Outcome: FRED reacts to "open Spotify" in milliseconds, saves its depth for when it's needed — and from here on, every change to him is remembered too.

Phase 13 – Reaching Outward
⏱ ~1 week
Tagline: "Hands beyond the LAN."
Goal: FRED knows what's happening in the world right now, not just what it was trained on.
Tasks:
  - websearch_handler() (DuckDuckGo / Brave) registered as a callable tool.
  - Weather and quick facts as the first proof that this pattern generalizes.
Outcome: FRED stops guessing and starts knowing.

Phase 14 – Hands on the Machine
⏱ ~1.5–2 weeks
Tagline: "This is my house. He should be able to move through it."
Goal: Real control of my own PC — not toy commands, actual command of the machine.
Tasks:
  - Window management, volume/brightness, clipboard, screenshots, process control.
  - File search, move, rename, read-on-request.
  - A confirmation gate before anything irreversible — FRED asks before it deletes or kills something. Trust, not recklessness.
Outcome: FRED isn't a chatbot with a few tricks. He's inside the machine with me.

Phase 15 – He Speaks First
⏱ ~1 week
Tagline: "Not just answering. Noticing."
Goal: FRED stops being purely reactive.
Tasks:
  - Background scheduler for reminders, timers, periodic checks.
  - Simple proactive triggers — "remind me in 20," "tell me when this file shows up."
  - A real notification voice/visual, not a silent log entry.
Outcome: FRED can interrupt me with something that matters, the way someone who actually pays attention would.

Phase 16 – A Face for the Mind
⏱ ~1–1.5 weeks
Tagline: "He should look alive."
Goal: The single biggest thing that makes this feel like JARVIS and not a terminal window.
Tasks:
  - Always-on-top HUD — idle / listening / thinking / speaking states, animated.
  - Visual confirmation when tools fire ("opening Chrome" flashes by).
  - A live transcript so I can see what he heard.
Outcome: Walking past my desk, FRED looks like he's there. Because he is.

Phase 17 – Eyes on the Screen
⏱ ~1.5 weeks
Tagline: "See what I see."
Goal: FRED can look at my screen and reason about it.
Tasks:
  - Screenshot + vision-capable model call for "what's this error" / "read this for me."
  - Skip webcam presence-detection — adds creep-factor with no real payoff for a personal-use machine.
Outcome: FRED can debug an error on sight, without me typing out what's already in front of both of us.

Phase 18 – Becoming Mine
⏱ ~1–2 weeks
Tagline: "Not a generic assistant wearing a JARVIS costume."
Goal: FRED sounds like FRED — specifically, consistently, and shaped by me.
Tasks:
  - Log real conversations locally (no cloud, no third party) as a style reference.
  - Iterate system_prompt.py against what actually feels off in daily use — cheap, fast, honest feedback loop.
  - Only reach for real fine-tuning (LoRA) once prompt iteration plateaus — not before.
Outcome: He's not impersonating an assistant. He's mine.

Phase 19 – The Mind Remembers, Selectively
⏱ ~1 week
Tagline: "Not a hoarder. A confidant."
Goal: Fix memory before it becomes a junk drawer of every "hello" ever said.
Tasks:
  - Consolidate old raw messages into compact, durable facts on a schedule.
  - Split memory into categories — who I am, what I prefer, what I'm working on — instead of one undifferentiated stream.
  - Let irrelevant, stale memories fade. Real memory forgets the unimportant on purpose.
Outcome: FRED remembers what matters about me, not just what was typed.

Phase 20 – Trusted to Run Himself
⏱ ~1.5–2 weeks
Tagline: "Built to live with, not to demo."
Goal: This stops being a project I open occasionally and becomes something running, always, in the background of my life.
Tasks:
  - Permission tiers — read-only runs free, anything destructive or irreversible asks first, always.
  - Secrets hygiene locked down for good (keys rotated, .env gitignored, nothing exposed) before this ever touches git.
  - Starts on boot, recovers from crashes, falls back to local inference gracefully if the cloud goes down.
  - One last personality + voice + visual pass — not for a demo audience, for me, every day.
Outcome: FRED isn't something I show people anymore. He's something I live with.


💡 What got cut, and why:
No "autonomous self-improvement," no "emotional intelligence dataset
fine-tuning," no enterprise-flavored fluff. Those are research directions
for a company, not steps toward a companion. What actually makes something
feel like JARVIS: it's always listening, it looks alive on screen, it can
touch my machine, it knows things in real time, and it remembers me
specifically. Phases 11-17 build that presence. 18-20 make him durable
enough, and personal enough, to actually keep.
